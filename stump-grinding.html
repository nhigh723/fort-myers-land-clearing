Stump Grinding in Fort Myers, FL

Stump grinding takes a leftover stump down below ground level so the spot is usable again — mowable, plantable, or buildable. A grinder chews through the stump in well under an hour for most residential sizes, and the ground disturbance is a fraction of what digging a stump out would cause.

Home › Stump Grinding

How it works

The machine is a stump grinder: a heavy cutting wheel that spins against the stump while the operator sweeps it side to side. It shaves the wood down several inches below the surrounding ground, leaving a mound of chips where the stump was. The roots underneath stay where they are and rot away over time.

A typical residential stump takes half an hour to an hour. The chips can go with the crew or stay on the lot as mulch — worth deciding before you get the quote, because haul-away changes the price.

Grinding vs. pulling the stump out

People often use these interchangeably, but they are different jobs with different costs and different results:

Stump grindingStump removal (pulling)
What happensStump ground below grade; roots stayStump and root ball dug out entirely
Ground disturbanceMinimal — a shallow depressionSignificant — a hole to backfill
CostLowerHigher (bigger machine, more time, backfill)
Best forLawns, landscaping, general cleanupFoundations, pools, septic, utilities

Grinding is the right call for most Fort Myers homeowners. Pulling only becomes necessary when something has to go into the ground in that exact spot — a footing, a pool shell, a septic line. If you are building, check the plans with your builder before booking either one.

Cheapest when it's part of a bigger job

If a crew is already on the lot clearing it, adding stump grinding to the same visit almost always beats the price of a separate trip — the machines and the crew are already there. Clearing packages that include stump grinding run around $750 per acre in Southwest Florida, against $1,500–$2,500 per acre for full cut-and-haul clearing. What sets the price

Grinding is usually priced stump by stump. What you pay depends on:

  • Size — the single biggest factor. A foot-wide stump is a quick job. A three-foot live oak stump — and Fort Myers' older neighborhoods have some very large oaks — is a much longer one.
  • How many — the per-stump price drops fast on multi-stump jobs, because the trip, setup, and mobilization are shared across all of them.
  • Wood type and condition — dense hardwood grinds slowly; soft or rotted wood goes fast. A solid live oak stump takes noticeably longer than a rotted palm stump of the same size.
  • Getting the machine there — an open lot is easy. A fenced backyard with a narrow side gate may need a smaller grinder or more time, and in-town lots with tight side yards are the classic example.
  • What happens to the chips — hauling them away costs more than leaving them in a pile. Decide which you want before the quote.
  • What's buried nearby — grinding near underground lines needs care and a Florida 811 locate first. A professional builds that into the job.

Why not leave the stump alone?

Left in place, stumps cause problems that get worse with time in this climate: they draw termites and other pests, they send up new shoots (Brazilian pepper is famous for regrowing from a cut stump), they block mowing and building, and they make a lot look unfinished. On a lot you are selling or building on, stumps read as work half done. Grinding is the least expensive way to close the job out properly.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Is the price per stump or for the whole job — and how do you measure diameter?
  • How far below grade do you grind?
  • Do the chips get hauled away, or do they stay on site?
  • Do you handle the Florida 811 locate before you start?
  • If I'm clearing the lot too, is there a bundled price for grinding with the clearing?
  • Do you grind surface roots as well, or just the stump itself?
