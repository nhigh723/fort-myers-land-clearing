Lot Clearing in Fort Myers, FL

Full lot clearing in Fort Myers typically costs $1,500–$2,500 per acre. Everything in the clearing area comes down and leaves the lot — trees, brush, stumps, and debris — so the ground is clean and ready for surveying, site prep, and construction.

What a full clearing covers

Full clearing — cut-and-haul — is the complete job. A crew with saws, a skid steer or excavator, and haul trucks takes everything out of the clearing area and removes it from the property. What that normally means:

  • Trees felled and cut — every tree in the clearing area, including large mature trees.
  • Brush and undergrowth removed — palmetto, pepper, melaleuca, and saplings taken down to bare ground.
  • Debris loaded and hauled — nothing stays behind. The lot ends up clean rather than covered in mulch or chips.
  • Stumps dealt with — pulled, ground, or left in place, per what you agree in the quote. This choice is one of the biggest price variables on the job.
  • Rough cleanup — the ground ends up clear and walkable, so a surveyor or a site prep and grading crew can start straight away.

Hauling is what makes full clearing cost more than forestry mulching — trucks, fuel, and disposal add up. But the result is a genuinely clean lot, which is what most building projects require.

When full clearing is the right call

  • Building a home — the most common reason in Fort Myers, from infill parcels near downtown to new lots in south-side subdivisions. A builder needs clean ground for surveying, footings, and access.
  • Stumps have to go — foundations, pools, septic systems, and underground utilities can't go in where stumps are still in the ground.
  • Storm damage — a lot full of downed trees, broken limbs, and scattered debris needs the material separated and hauled, not mulched where it fell.
  • Debris buried in the growth — old fencing, concrete, dumped material, or storm wreckage that can't be mulched and has to leave on a truck.
  • Selling or developing — a clean, visible lot shows far better than an overgrown one. Buyers and builders can see what they are getting.

Mulching or full clearing? A quick way to decide

If the lot only needs opening up and the ground can stay under a mulch layer, forestry mulching is cheaper and faster. If you're building, need the stumps out, or want the lot genuinely clean, full clearing is the answer. Plenty of Fort Myers homeowners do both in sequence — mulch first to see the lot, then full clearing and grading when the build is ready.

Permits, locates, and protected trees

Three things to settle before a crew starts:

  • The right permit, from the right office. Clearing permits come from the City of Fort Myers inside city limits and from Lee County in unincorporated areas. They are typically required for larger lots, lots near wetlands or waterways, and clearing tied to new construction. Contractors usually handle the paperwork — confirm it is in the quote, and confirm which jurisdiction your lot falls under.
  • Florida 811 locates. Underground utilities must be marked before any digging or stump pulling. Call 811 or have your contractor do it — it is free, and it is required. Lots with overhead lines nearby also need coordination with the electric provider; FPL serves most of Fort Myers, though some unincorporated pockets use a different provider.
  • Protected trees and waterways. Live oaks, sabal palms, and vegetation along wetland edges have protections under city, county, and state rules. Mangroves on the river and canals are protected statewide and may only be trimmed by a qualified professional. A reputable contractor spots these before cutting. Lots bordering water, a wetland, or a preserve get extra review.

How long it takes

The clearing itself usually runs one to three days for a quarter-acre to one-acre residential lot, depending on density and whether stumps are pulled or ground. Permitting happens before work starts and adds its own time, and grading adds more if the lot needs a building pad afterward. Wet-season rain (May–October) can push schedules back, and heavy storm debris adds time over a lot with growth alone — ask your contractor how they handle weather delays and debris separation.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Is the price per acre or for the whole job — and is hauling in it?
  • Do stumps get pulled, ground, or left? What does each option cost?
  • Who handles the permit — city or county — and the Florida 811 locates?
  • How do you deal with protected trees, mangroves, or wetland edges on the lot?
  • What happens to material that can't go in a normal haul — concrete, fencing, storm debris?
  • Is site prep and grading part of this job, or a separate one?
