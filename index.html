Land clearing services in Fort Myers

Fort Myers lots range from small in-town parcels to multi-acre tracts on the east side, and the right service depends on the lot. These are the five kinds of work most jobs need — or describe your lot and a local pro will recommend the fit.

Forestry Mulching

One machine shreds trees, brush, and undergrowth into mulch where it stands — no trucks, no hauling, and the ground stays covered and stable. The standard answer for lots swallowed by Brazilian pepper and melaleuca.

Typically $800–$1,200 / acre

Lot Clearing

The complete job: trees down, brush out, stumps dealt with, and every bit of debris hauled off the lot. What a building site needs before surveying starts — and the right call when a lot has storm debris or dumped material in the mix.

Typically $1,500–$2,500 / acre

Brush Clearing

Bush hogging for lighter growth — tall grass, palmetto, and saplings on a lot that's grown up but isn't wooded yet. The cheapest way to take a lot back, keep a vacant parcel in shape, or finally see what you're working with.

Lowest-cost clearing option

Stump Grinding

Takes stumps down below grade so the ground is usable for mowing, landscaping, or building. Often done alongside a clearing job — and worth doing after any tree removal, since pepper stumps in particular sprout back fast.

Describe Your Lot

Why Fort Myers lots are different

Fort Myers is the county seat of Lee County and the oldest city in Southwest Florida, and its lots reflect that: small in-town parcels near downtown, suburban lots across the south side, larger acreage to the east, and riverfront and canal-front property along the Caloosahatchee. A few things about clearing here are worth knowing before you hire anyone:

  • City limits change the paperwork. Inside the City of Fort Myers, clearing permits come from the city; in unincorporated Lee County — which covers many "Fort Myers" addresses — they come from the county. Two lots a mile apart can have different rules. A good contractor checks which jurisdiction the lot is in before starting.
  • Water is never far away. Riverfront lots, canal lots, and low-lying parcels near the Caloosahatchee need extra care: mangroves are protected statewide, wetland edges have setbacks, and grading has to respect drainage and flood rules. Clearing near water is slower and more regulated than clearing a dry inland lot.
  • Storm debris is part of the job here. Hurricanes leave Fort Myers lots full of downed trees, broken limbs, and scattered debris. Clearing a storm-damaged lot is different from clearing an overgrown one — the debris has to be separated and hauled, and hidden damage (leaning trees, damaged fencing) shows up once the growth comes off.
  • Invasives grow back fast. Brazilian pepper and melaleuca colonize any lot left alone for a season or two, and saw palmetto thickets hide stumps, debris, and uneven ground. A quote that assumes light brush will be wrong on most overgrown Fort Myers lots.
  • Sandy soil, flat ground. The area's sandy soil drains quickly but ruts easily under heavy machines, especially in the wet season (May–October). Flat lots also mean drainage has to be created, not assumed — water has nowhere to go on its own.

Good to know before you get a quote

Three things drive your price more than anything: (1) how thick the growth is, (2) whether stumps come out or get ground, and (3) whether debris is hauled off or mulched in place. A quote that doesn't ask about all three isn't a real quote — and on a riverfront or storm-damaged lot, access and debris add a fourth variable.

Utilities near the work

Florida 811 locates are required before any digging. Lots near overhead lines need coordination with the electric provider — FPL serves most of Fort Myers, though some unincorporated pockets use a different provider, so confirm by address. This is normally part of the job — check that it's in the quote.

Common questions

How much does land clearing cost in Fort Myers, FL?

In Southwest Florida, forestry mulching typically runs $800–$1,200 per acre and traditional cut-and-haul clearing runs $1,500–$2,500 per acre, with light mulching packages starting around $450 per acre. What you actually pay depends on how dense the growth is, the terrain, access to the lot, and whether stumps and hauling are part of the job. See the full cost guide.

Do I need a permit to clear land in Fort Myers?

It depends on the lot's location. Clearing permits come from the City of Fort Myers inside city limits and from Lee County in unincorporated areas. A permit is typically needed for larger lots, lots near wetlands or waterways, and clearing tied to new construction. Contractors usually handle the permit — ask whether that's included in your quote.

What is forestry mulching, and is it right for my lot?

Forestry mulching is a single-machine method: a tracked mulcher shreds trees, brush, and undergrowth and lays the material back down as mulch, with no hauling and little ground disturbance. It suits overgrown lots and Brazilian pepper or melaleuca removal, and lots where a stable, mulch-covered ground is fine. It does not suit lots where stumps must come out or a clean graded building pad is needed. More on forestry mulching.

How long does it take to clear a lot in Fort Myers?

Most quarter-acre to one-acre residential lots take one to a few days to clear, depending on growth density and whether stumps, hauling, and grading are included. Wet-season rain (May–October) can slow work on saturated sandy soil, and riverfront or canal lots can take longer because of access limits and protected-vegetation checks.
