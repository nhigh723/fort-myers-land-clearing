Land Clearing Costs in Fort Myers, FL

Clearing one acre in Fort Myers typically costs $800–$1,200 for forestry mulching or $1,500–$2,500 for traditional cut-and-haul. Below is every service priced, the factors that push a quote up or down, and how to tell a fair price from a bad one.

First, the honest framing: these are typical ranges, not quotes.

No two lots cost the same. The numbers below are typical Southwest Florida figures for residential work, drawn from published market pricing. What you actually pay turns on your lot's density, terrain, access, water frontage, and what needs to happen with stumps and debris. Use these figures to judge quotes — not as a price list.

What a typical Fort Myers lot runs

Fort Myers residential lots are mostly a quarter-acre to one acre. Multiply the per-acre ranges out and most jobs land roughly here:

That table is simple multiplication, so read it as a rough sense-check. Small jobs often carry a minimum charge, larger lots often get a slightly lower per-acre rate, and tight in-town parcels can run higher per acre than the table suggests because access is harder and setup time is the same. It is a starting point, not a price list.

What pushes the price up — and down

How dense the growth is — the biggest lever

Light palmetto and grass go fast. A decade of unmanaged pepper, melaleuca, and tangled undergrowth takes far longer and wears equipment harder. A lot that was cut last year costs noticeably less to clear than one that has sat untouched for ten. On a lot that's been growing unchecked for years, budget for the top of the range — or beyond it.

What happens to the stumps

Leaving stumps at grade adds nothing to the bill. Grinding them below grade adds a moderate amount. Pulling them out with the root ball costs the most — bigger equipment, more time, and holes to backfill. Settle this before you collect quotes, or you will end up comparing different jobs against each other.

Hauling vs. mulching in place

Trucks, fuel, disposal fees, and time — hauling is what accounts for most of the gap between mulching ($800–$1,200/acre) and cut-and-haul ($1,500–$2,500/acre). If the lot can take a mulch layer, mulching is the single biggest saving available.

Access and terrain

A flat lot with a wide entrance is the easy case. Narrow access, a tight in-town side yard, wet low spots, or no road frontage all slow the work and can force smaller machines. Wet-season ground (May–October) makes everything slower on sandy soil. Riverfront and canal lots are often the hardest of all — access is usually limited to one side, and the water side can't be used for staging equipment or debris.

Storm debris mixed into the growth

Downed trees, broken limbs, and scattered debris from past storms can't go through a mulcher as-is. They have to be separated and hauled, which adds time and truckloads. A lot with heavy storm debris costs more than a lot with growth alone, and a reputable contractor will point that out after a site visit instead of surprising you halfway through the job.

Fill, if the job includes a building pad

Once site prep enters the picture, fill quantities drive the cost. Some lots need almost none; low-lying parcels near the river or canals can need many loads. Get the fill estimate in writing — it is the most common source of price surprises in site prep.

Included or extra? Check the fine print

Before you compare two quotes, check whether each one covers:

  • Permitting — city or county, depending on where the lot sits. Sometimes in the price, sometimes billed separately.
  • Florida 811 locates — normally handled by the contractor, but worth confirming.
  • Stump work — left, ground, or pulled? And ground to what depth?
  • Debris — hauled away, mulched in place, or left for you to deal with?
  • Cleanup — is the lot left walkable and clear, or is final cleanup an extra?
  • Grading — rough grade, finish grade, or nothing at all?

Two quotes hundreds of dollars apart can both be fair if they describe different scopes. The cheapest number is only cheaper when it buys the same work.

Comparing quotes without getting fooled

Get at least two, and make sure each one answers the same questions: what is included, how stumps are handled, whether hauling and permits are in the price, and how long the job takes. A quote that never asks about your lot's density, access, and plans is a guess — and guesses tend to get revised upward once the crew is on site.

Ways to bring the cost down

  • Mulch instead of haul wherever the lot allows it — the largest single saving.
  • Bundle stump grinding into the clearing job — one crew, one trip, lower per-stump cost.
  • Clear in the dry season when the schedule allows — wet-season delays add time and money.
  • Clear only what you need — building on half the lot? You may not need the whole acre cleared.
  • Keep the lot maintained afterward — a seasonal brush cut costs far less than letting the lot grow back into a mulching job.
