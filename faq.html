Land Clearing FAQ — Fort Myers, FL

Straight answers to the questions Fort Myers homeowners ask most about land clearing — costs, city vs. county permits, mulching vs. clearing, stumps, storm debris, riverfront lots, and how quotes work.

How much does land clearing cost in Fort Myers, FL?

In Southwest Florida, forestry mulching typically runs $800–$1,200 per acre and traditional cut-and-haul clearing runs $1,500–$2,500 per acre, with light mulching packages starting around $450 per acre. What you actually pay depends on how dense the growth is, the terrain, access to the lot, and whether stumps and hauling are part of the job. See the full cost guide.

Do I need a permit to clear land in Fort Myers?

It depends on the lot's location. Clearing permits come from the City of Fort Myers inside city limits and from Lee County in unincorporated areas. A permit is typically needed for larger lots, lots near wetlands or waterways, and clearing tied to new construction. Contractors usually handle the permit — ask whether that's included in your quote.

What is forestry mulching, and is it right for my lot?

Forestry mulching is a single-machine method: a tracked mulcher shreds trees, brush, and undergrowth and lays the material back down as mulch, with no hauling and little ground disturbance. It suits overgrown lots and Brazilian pepper or melaleuca removal, and lots where a stable, mulch-covered ground is fine. It does not suit lots where stumps must come out or a clean graded building pad is needed. More on forestry mulching.

How long does it take to clear a lot in Fort Myers?

Most quarter-acre to one-acre residential lots take one to a few days to clear, depending on growth density and whether stumps, hauling, and grading are included. Wet-season rain (May–October) can slow work on saturated sandy soil, and riverfront or canal lots can take longer because of access limits and protected-vegetation checks.

What is the difference between forestry mulching and traditional lot clearing?

Mulching shreds growth into mulch on site — cheaper at $800–$1,200 per acre and faster, but stumps remain and the ground ends up covered in mulch. Traditional clearing cuts everything down and hauls the debris away at $1,500–$2,500 per acre, leaving a clean lot ready to build on. Mulching fits lots that just need opening up; full clearing fits building sites and lots with storm debris. Compare both in the lot clearing guide.

Do stumps need to come out, or can they stay?

It depends on the lot's future. Stumps can stay if the lot is only being opened up. For lawns and landscaping they should be ground below grade, and where a foundation, pool, septic system, or utility line will go, they need to be pulled out entirely. Grinding costs less than pulling and is the right choice for most homeowners. More on stump grinding.

What is Florida 811, and why does it matter for clearing?

Florida 811 is the free service that marks underground utilities before anyone digs. A locate is required before stump pulling, grading, or any work that breaks ground. Contractors usually request the locate — confirm it is covered before work begins.

Can land clearing happen during the wet season?

Yes, but May–October work can run slower. Heavy rain saturates Fort Myers' sandy soil, which ruts easily under tracked machines, and storms can push a job back several days. The dry season is normally faster and cleaner, though a good operator works year-round and will say honestly when conditions call for a wait.

Are there trees I am not allowed to cut in Fort Myers?

Yes. Some native trees — live oaks and sabal palms, for example — and vegetation along wetland edges have protections under city, county, and state rules. Mangroves along the river and canals are protected statewide and may only be trimmed by a qualified professional. A reputable contractor will identify protected trees and wetland areas before cutting, and lots bordering water or a preserve get extra review.

What happens to the debris after clearing?

It depends on the method. Mulching leaves shredded material on the lot as a mulch layer. Traditional clearing loads debris and hauls it off-site. Brush clearing leaves cut material to break down. Storm debris — downed trees, broken limbs, fencing — is separated and hauled. Debris handling is part of the quote, so check whether hauling and disposal are included.

How do I know if my lot needs brush clearing or full clearing?

Walk the lot. If you can push through the growth, brush clearing (bush hogging) will probably handle it. If pepper and palmetto form a wall you can't get through, the job needs forestry mulching or full clearing. A few photos are usually enough for a local professional to tell you which one fits.

Does clearing include grading and a building pad?

No, not on its own. Clearing takes the vegetation off; site prep and grading shape the ground for building — a compacted pad, drainage, driveway base, and finish grade. Homeowners often book both as one project with the same crew. Full site prep with grading and haul runs around $1,100 per acre in Southwest Florida.
