Site Prep & Grading in Fort Myers, FL

Site prep is what turns a cleared lot into a build-ready one: a compacted building pad, drainage that sends water away from the home, a driveway base, and a finish grade. Full site prep with grading and haul runs around $1,100 per acre in Southwest Florida — and on Fort Myers' flat, sandy ground, the drainage work matters as much as the clearing did.

What site prep covers

Clearing takes the vegetation off. Site prep shapes the ground for everything that comes next. A typical job covers:

  • The building pad — the house footprint gets leveled, filled, and compacted to the elevation in your building plans. Everything else sits on this.
  • Drainage and swales — the lot is shaped so rainwater runs away from the home toward the street or a drainage area. On Fort Myers' flat ground, drainage has to be built — water has nowhere to go by itself.
  • Driveway base — the driveway area is graded and based with compacted material, so it holds up under vehicles instead of rutting into sand.
  • Finish grade — the lot is smoothed out to its final elevations, so sod, landscaping, or construction can begin.
  • Fill and haul-off — low spots get filled, excess material leaves on a truck. On flat Fort Myers lots, the fill estimate is a large part of the quote.

Flat ground, sandy soil, and water nearby

Fort Myers sits on flat, sandy ground beside the Caloosahatchee River, and that changes site prep in a few practical ways:

  • Drainage comes first. Sand drains quickly — until a heavy wet-season storm overwhelms it. On a flat lot, swales and positive drainage away from the home are what keep the lot dry in August. Riverfront and canal lots need even more care, because the water table runs high and there is little margin for error.
  • Compaction counts for more. Sand does not hold its shape the way clay does. A pad has to be compacted in lifts, or it settles after the slab goes down.
  • Fresh grade washes out fast. One hard rain can erode a newly graded sandy lot. Good operators grade with that in mind and stabilize exposed areas.
  • Fill needs vary wildly. Some Fort Myers lots sit close to finished elevation already; others — especially low-lying parcels near water — need a lot of fill to reach the required pad height. That difference can run into thousands of dollars, so get the fill estimate in writing.
  • Flood rules can set the pad height. Lots in or near flood zones may have a required finished-floor elevation. That number comes from your survey and building plans, and it drives how much fill the job needs — find it out before you collect quotes, not after.

Book clearing and site prep together

Most homeowners schedule lot clearing and site prep as a single project, often with the same crew. That usually costs less than two separate jobs — the machines are already on site, and the operator can grade as they clear. When you request a quote, say what you're building and whether you need a pad. It changes the price and the equipment.

Permits and surveys

  • Plans set the pad. The pad's elevation and footprint come from your building plans and survey. Make sure the grading contractor has them before work starts.
  • City or county approval. Site prep tied to new construction usually falls under the building permit, but standalone grading may need its own approval — and whether the city or the county issues it depends on where the lot sits. Have your contractor confirm.
  • Florida 811 locates. Any digging for drainage, utilities, or fill work needs underground utilities marked first. It is free and required.
  • Wetlands, waterways, and setbacks. Grading near a wetland edge, a canal, or a property line has rules. A good operator raises setback and wetland issues before moving dirt.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Is this quote based on my building plans and survey, or is it a general estimate?
  • How much fill is included, and what does additional fill cost per load?
  • Is compaction tested, or done by experience?
  • How is drainage handled — swales, culverts, grading alone, or a combination?
  • Is the driveway base part of the price, or separate?
  • Who handles permits — city or county — and is that cost in the quote?
