Forestry Mulching in Fort Myers, FL

Forestry mulching in Fort Myers typically costs $800–$1,200 per acre. One machine shreds trees, brush, and undergrowth into mulch where it stands — no trucks, no burn piles, and far less ground disturbance than cutting and hauling everything away.

Home › Forestry Mulching

How it works

A forestry mulcher is a tracked machine carrying a rotating drum of cutting teeth. It drives straight through the growth, shredding trees and brush up to several inches thick and dropping the shredded material back down as a mulch layer. One operator and one machine can take an overgrown residential lot down in a day or two — the same lot would take a crew, trucks, and several haul trips the traditional way.

That single-pass method is why mulching is usually the cheapest way to clear a heavily overgrown lot. Around Fort Myers, where Brazilian pepper, melaleuca, and saw palmetto can close over a vacant parcel in a season or two, mulching is the usual first recommendation — on the larger lots east of town, in the south-side subdivisions where vacant parcels sit between built homes, and anywhere the growth has had years to take hold.

Where mulching shines

  • Overgrown vacant lots — years of unmanaged growth, dense palmetto, and invasive trees that have closed over the lot.
  • Pepper and melaleuca takedowns — both spread aggressively across Southwest Florida, and mulching is the most cost-effective way to knock them back.
  • Ground that needs to stay put — the mulch layer holds sandy soil in place, suppresses regrowth, and breaks down into the ground over time.
  • Working around trees you want to keep — mulching is selective, so the operator can clear around the live oaks or palms you want to save.
  • Firebreaks and access paths — mulching is the standard way to cut fire lines and open routes through heavy growth, including on larger east Fort Myers parcels.

What you're left with afterward

Mulching takes growth down to ground level, but stumps and roots below the surface stay where they are. The lot ends up under a mulch layer — good for erosion control, but not a clean building surface. If you're building, plan on site prep and grading afterward, and possibly stump grinding where stumps sit in the way of the build.

When mulching is the wrong tool

Mulching doesn't fit every lot. Traditional lot clearing is the better choice when:

  • Stumps need to come out completely — under a foundation, a pool, or underground utilities.
  • The lot has debris, old fencing, concrete, or dumped material tangled into the growth. A mulcher can't process it; it has to be picked out and hauled.
  • Storm damage left downed trees and tangled limbs across the lot — that material usually needs separating and hauling before or instead of mulching.
  • You need a clean, graded building pad immediately and don't want a mulch layer to work around.
  • Large mature trees have to come down — mulching handles small-to-medium trees; big ones need a saw crew first.

Questions to ask a mulching operator

  • Is the quote per acre or for the whole job — and what exactly does it cover?
  • Are stumps ground, left at grade, or an extra charge?
  • Who handles the permit — the city or the county, depending on the lot?
  • How do you handle Florida 811 locates and power lines on or near the lot?
  • What happens to material that can't be mulched — fencing, concrete, storm debris?
  • How long will the job take, and what happens if wet-season rain delays it?

Local tip: watch the season

Fort Myers' wet season runs May–October. A tracked mulcher chews ruts into waterlogged sand, and a stretch of heavy rain can set a job back a few days. If your schedule has room, the dry season is usually the faster, cleaner window — but a good operator works year-round and will say honestly when conditions call for a wait. On riverfront or canal lots, high water after storms can limit access too.
